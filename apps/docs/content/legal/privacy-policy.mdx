## What we collect

### When you visit qrdx.dev

- **Request data.** Your browser sends your IP address, user agent and the page requested. Our host, Vercel, processes these to deliver the page and protect the Service. We read the country from Vercel's IP-country header to show prices in Indian rupees or US dollars. We don't store your IP address with your visit.
- **Browser storage.** Settings such as your theme, editor state and chosen currency are saved in your browser. The [Cookie Policy](/legal/cookie-policy) lists every item.
- **Product analytics, only if you allow it.** PostHog records the pages you view, the clicks you make and the features you use. If you are signed in, these events are linked to your email address and name.
- **Feedback widget, only if you allow it.** Featurebase loads its widget, and what you type into it goes to Featurebase.

### Static codes and free tools

Static codes you make in the editor are generated in your browser. Their content isn't sent to us unless you save the design, use an AI feature, or use the [Image API](/docs/developers/image-api), where the content is part of the request. The QR scanner keeps its history in your browser only. The scan checker sends the link you check to our server and to Google Web Risk to test it; we don't store the link.

### Your account

- **Sign-in.** You sign in with Google, GitHub or a one-time code we email you. With Google or GitHub, we receive your name, email address, profile picture and your account ID at that provider, plus the tokens the provider issues for signing in. With an email code, we receive only your email address. The code expires after 5 minutes, and we store it only as a hash until it is used. QRdx has no passwords.
- **Sessions.** A session cookie keeps you signed in. With each session we store the IP address and user agent of the device that signed in, to secure your account.
- **Settings.** Your preferences and notification settings: which events you want, and the email addresses or Slack and Discord webhook URLs they go to.

### What you create

- **Dynamic codes:** destination and fallback URLs, title, notes, design, folder and tags, routing rules, UTM settings, tracking pixel IDs, schedules and scan limits. A code's password is stored only as a hash. We keep an edit history of each code.
- **Hosted pages:** their content and earlier versions, which can include contact details you publish, such as a vCard.
- **Files** you upload, for example images, PDFs, audio or video for your pages.
- **Saved themes**, and **community themes** you publish. Published themes are public and show your name and profile picture.
- **Custom domains**, **webhook endpoints** and their signing secrets, and data you **import** from other services.

### Teams

The team's name and logo, its members and their roles, and invitations: the invitee's email address, the role offered and who sent it. What you create inside a team belongs to the team. See [Teams](/docs/teams).

### Scans of dynamic codes (End User data)

| We record | We don't record |
| --- | --- |
| Time of the scan | IP address |
| Country, region and city, looked up from the IP address by Vercel | Full user agent string |
| Device type, operating system and browser, read from the user agent | Precise location |
| Referring website (host name only, never the full address) | Cookies or device identifiers |
| Preferred language, from the browser's language setting | |
| Which A/B variant was served, if the owner set up a split | |
| A visitor hash | |

The **visitor hash** is a salted SHA-256 hash of the IP address, the user agent and the date (UTC). Because the date is part of it, the hash changes every day. It lets the code's owner count unique visitors per day without anyone storing the IP address, and it can't be used to follow someone from one day to the next.

**Bots aren't counted.** Known bots, link previews and browser prefetches are skipped. Repeated scans of one code from one IP address stop counting after 20 a minute; the code still redirects.

**Password-protected codes** set a cookie on the scanner's device for 6 hours after the right password is entered. **Conversion tracking**, if the owner turns it on, adds a signed `qrdx_sid` parameter to the destination link, and we store the conversions the owner reports back (name, value, currency and any details they send).

### Hosted pages and forms (End User data)

- **Page views and clicks.** A cookieless beacon records the event type, which block or link was used, the time and the visitor hash.
- **Form responses.** We store the answers, which can include a name, email address, phone number, ratings, choices or consent ticks, with the time and the visitor hash. A hidden honeypot field and, where it's enabled, Cloudflare Turnstile filter out spam. Turnstile sends the visitor's IP address to Cloudflare to check it.
- **Notifications.** Owners can receive responses by email, which we send through Resend, and can send events to their own webhooks, Slack or Discord.
- **Owner's tracking pixels.** An owner can add Google Analytics 4, Google Tag Manager, Meta, TikTok or LinkedIn pixels to their pages. In the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and wherever a visitor's country is unknown, these wait for the visitor's consent. Elsewhere they respect Global Privacy Control. The visitor's choice is stored in their browser. The owner is responsible for their pixels.
- **Embedded content.** If the owner adds a map or a video, it is loaded from Google Maps, YouTube (in its privacy-enhanced mode), Vimeo or Loom, and those providers receive the visitor's request.

QRdx's own analytics and feedback widget never load on hosted pages.

### AI features

Your prompts, attached images and, for AI art, the content of the code are sent to Google (Gemini) or fal.ai to generate the result. We store a usage record (which model, how many tokens or credits, when). We don't store your prompts or images on our servers. The [AI Features](/legal/ai-features) notice has the details.

### Integrations and imports

When you connect an integration, such as Dub or Google Drive, we store the access and refresh tokens it issues, encrypted with AES-256-GCM, together with the permissions you granted and account details like the workspace name. Data then moves between QRdx and that service only as you direct.

### API, CLI and MCP server

For OAuth apps and access tokens we store a hash of each token, its scopes and its expiry date, never the token itself. We count your requests to apply your plan's daily limit.

### Billing

[Polar](https://polar.sh) is the merchant of record for paid plans. Polar, and Stripe for card payments, collect your payment details, billing address and tax ID. We receive your plan, its price and currency, billing period dates, subscription status, your Polar customer ID and, if you give one, your reason for cancelling. We never see your full card number.

### Emails

We send transactional email only, through Resend: sign-in codes, team invitations, form response notifications, first-scan and milestone alerts, and weekly digests if you turn them on. Alerts and digests include a one-click unsubscribe link. We don't send marketing email.

### Abuse reports and safety

The [report form](/report-abuse) collects the reported link, a reason, optional details and an optional email address, plus a visitor hash. It uses Cloudflare Turnstile. Your IP address is used to limit how many reports can be sent from one connection. Destinations of dynamic codes are checked with Google Web Risk and offline checks. Staff actions on accounts, codes and files are recorded in an internal audit log.

### Support

What you send us when you email for help.

We don't make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects by automated means alone. Automated safety checks can hold a new account's code for review or switch off a code whose link is listed as malware or phishing; a person reviews those cases.

## Who we share it with

- **Subprocessors** that run parts of the Service for us, such as hosting, the database, email and billing. They are listed with their locations on the [Subprocessors](/legal/subprocessors) page.
- **Integrations you connect.** When you link a service, data goes to it because you asked for that. Those services are not our subprocessors, and their own terms apply.
- **Your team.** Members can see and manage the team's content according to their role.
- **The public.** Your codes' destinations, your hosted pages and the community themes you publish are public by design.
- **Authorities and others, when we must.** We share data if the law requires it, or where it is needed to protect people, QRdx or our users from harm or fraud.
- **A buyer or successor.** If QRdx is merged, acquired or its assets are sold, data may move to the new owner, who must keep honouring this policy. We'll tell you if that happens.

We don't sell personal data, and we don't share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.

## International transfers

Our main infrastructure (Vercel for hosting and Neon for the database) is in the United States, and some subprocessors work from other countries. When personal data from the EEA, the UK or Switzerland goes to a country without an adequacy decision, we rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (with the UK Addendum and the Swiss amendments where needed) or on the provider's certification under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Data from India may be transferred abroad except to countries the Government of India restricts.

## How we protect it

- Every connection to QRdx uses HTTPS.
- You sign in through Google or GitHub, so QRdx stores no passwords.
- API and OAuth access tokens are stored only as SHA-256 hashes. Integration tokens are encrypted with AES-256-GCM. Code passwords are stored as hashes.
- Scanner IP addresses are never written to the database (see [scans](#scans-of-dynamic-codes-end-user-data)).
- Webhook payloads are signed so you can check they came from us.
- Rate limits, bot filtering and Cloudflare Turnstile protect forms and public endpoints.
- Only staff accounts can open the admin tools, and every staff action there is logged.

No system is perfectly secure. If we learn of a breach that affects your personal data, we'll tell you and the relevant authorities as the law requires. To report a vulnerability, see [Security](/legal/security).
