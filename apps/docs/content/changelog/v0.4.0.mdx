## A passkey can be your second step

Two-step verification now works with a passkey as well as an authenticator app. After you sign in with an email code, Google, GitHub or single sign-on, your browser's passkey prompt opens on its own; **Use an authenticator code** and **Use a backup code** are right below it. [How it works](/docs/help/privacy-and-security/two-step-verification).

- Turn it on in **Settings** → **Profile & security**: pick **Passkey** or **Authenticator app**, then save your backup codes. You can set up both.
- Adding a passkey while two-step verification is off turns it on. Removing your last method turns it off, and QRdx tells you before you confirm.
- Already have a passkey? Settings shows **Your passkey is ready to be your second step** with a **Turn on** button. Nothing changes until you click it.
- **Don't ask again on this device for 30 days** works with the passkey step too.
- Passkeys, the authenticator app and backup codes now live together under **Two-step verification**.

On the sign-in page, **Continue with passkey** has moved to the bottom of the list and still signs you in on its own. The email field no longer suggests passkeys.

## Confirm it's you

Changing your email, deleting your account, changing your two-step verification or passkeys, and changing a workspace's single sign-on connection now ask you to confirm it's you first. With two-step verification on, use your passkey, an authenticator code or a backup code; without it, a code QRdx emails you. One confirmation covers 10 minutes of changes. This replaces the codes and recent-sign-in checks those actions asked for before.

## New sign-in alerts

When a browser or device signs in to your account for the first time, we email you with the device, rough location, IP address, time and how it signed in, and a **Review security** button. With two-step verification on, the email comes once the second step is done. You get up to 10 a day. The first sign-in to your account after this update records that browser without an email. After that, any browser QRdx hasn't seen sends one, including browsers you used before the update. [More about these emails](/docs/help/privacy-and-security/account-security#emails-about-your-accounts-security).

## Security emails say where and when

Every security email (two-step verification changes, passkeys added or removed, email changes, new sign-ins and confirmation codes) now shows the device and browser, rough location, IP address and time, in your local time zone where we know it. Sign-in codes say roughly where they were asked for, and every code email reminds you that QRdx never asks for a code by phone, chat or email.
