QR Code Monkey and QRdx solve the same core job: make a good-looking static QR code for free, with no account. Both encode your content directly in the code, so the codes never expire and have no scan limits. The differences are in the details around that job.

## Who each tool is for

QR Code Monkey suits anyone who needs a branded code quickly from a simple web form. It supports linear and radial gradients, custom body and eye shapes, logo uploads up to 2 MB, and explicitly allows commercial use.

QRdx suits the same people, plus teams that want PDF output with their design intact, an ad-free editor, or a way to generate the same styled codes inside their own product through the `qrdx` React package.

## Where QR Code Monkey is the better choice

- You need **EPS** files. QR Code Monkey offers EPS (for plain codes); QRdx does not export EPS.
- Your team already uses it and you only need PNG or SVG.
- You want its built-in logo gallery of common brand icons.

## Key differences

- **Export:** QR Code Monkey applies designs and logos to PNG and SVG only; its PDF and EPS files are classic black-and-white codes. QRdx keeps your full design in PNG, JPG, SVG and PDF.
- **Shapes:** QRdx offers 9 body patterns, 9 eye (corner frame) shapes and 11 eye-dot shapes, which can be mixed freely. QR Code Monkey offers body, eye frame and eye ball shape choices too.
- **Ads:** QR Code Monkey is ad-supported. QRdx shows no ads.
- **Editable codes:** neither makes dynamic codes on its free tool. QR Code Monkey points you to a separate paid product; QRdx lets you encode a [Dub](/integrations/dub) short link, so you can change the destination and see click analytics in Dub.
- **Code:** QRdx is open source (AGPL-3.0) and ships an npm package; QR Code Monkey does not.

## Switching from QR Code Monkey

1. Open the [QRdx playground](/playground) and enter the same URL or content.
2. Recreate your colors, gradient, shapes and logo. Save the result as a theme with a free account so you can reuse it.
3. Export SVG for print or PNG for screens, and test the code with two or three phone cameras before printing.

Codes you already printed from QR Code Monkey keep working, because static codes do not depend on either service.
