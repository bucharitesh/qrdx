Bitly is first a link shortener and link-management platform; QR codes are one feature among links, landing pages and analytics. QRdx does one thing: it makes well-designed QR codes, for free, with an open-source React library behind it.

## Who each tool is for

Bitly fits teams that already manage short links in Bitly and want QR codes tied to those links and their click history. Its plans are Free (2 QR codes a month, with ads), Core ($10/mo billed annually, 5 codes a month, 30 days of click and scan data), Growth ($29/mo billed annually, 10 codes, about 4 months of data) and Premium ($199/mo billed annually, 200 codes, 1 year of data plus city and device data).

QRdx fits anyone who needs more than a couple of codes, wants full control over the design, or wants to render codes inside their own app.

## Where Bitly is the better choice

- Your links already live in Bitly and you want QR scans and link clicks in one report.
- You want up to a year of scan history from the same vendor that hosts your links.
- Non-technical teammates need a single dashboard for links and codes.

QRdx does not shorten links itself. It integrates with [Dub](/integrations/dub), a link-management platform, so you can encode a Dub short link and track clicks there.

## Key differences

- **Volume:** Bitly meters QR codes per month; QRdx does not.
- **Design:** Bitly's free plan has limited colors and frames and no pattern customization. QRdx's free tier has every pattern, gradients, logos, six templates and PNG, JPG, SVG and PDF export.
- **Code model:** Bitly codes are tied to Bitly links. QRdx codes are static unless you choose to encode a short link.
- **Developers:** QRdx is also an npm package with TypeScript types, so you can render the same codes in your own app.

## Switching from Bitly

1. Keep Bitly codes that are already printed on an active account; they resolve through Bitly links.
2. Recreate your brand style in the [QRdx playground](/playground) and save it as a theme.
3. Encode the final URL directly, or a Dub link if you need to change it later.
4. Test each new code on a few phones before you print.
