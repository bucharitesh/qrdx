QR Code.io is a hosted dynamic QR platform. You sign up, start with a free trial, and continue on a monthly, quarterly or annual subscription that includes unlimited dynamic codes, unlimited scans, unlimited users and scan analytics (how many scans, when, where and on which device). QRdx works the other way around: it is free with no trial, makes static codes that never expire, and is open source.

## Who each tool is for

QR Code.io fits teams that want many content types behind editable codes, such as PDFs, menus, vCards, coupons, MP3s and lists of links, with hosting and analytics handled for them. Its homepage lists 18 types.

QRdx fits anyone who wants a well-designed code for a URL, Wi-Fi network, contact card, payment link or other content without signing up, and developers who want to render codes in their own app.

## Where QR Code.io is the better choice

- You need **hosted content** behind the code, such as a PDF or menu page, rather than a link to something you already host.
- You need **editable codes with scan analytics** for a team.
- You are happy to pay a subscription for an all-in-one tool.

## Key differences

- **Pricing:** QR Code.io is a subscription after a trial, and its plans page says subscriptions are not refunded, so check current prices there before you start. QRdx is free; Pro ($8/mo or $79/yr) only adds unlimited AI theme generation and saved themes.
- **Code model:** QR Code.io's dynamic codes depend on its service. QRdx codes are static unless you choose to encode a [Dub](/integrations/dub) short link.
- **Design:** QRdx gives separate control over 9 body patterns, 9 eye shapes and 11 eye-dot shapes, plus gradients, logos and six templates.
- **Openness:** QRdx is open source (AGPL-3.0) with an npm package and CLI.

## Switching from QR Code.io

1. Find every printed code that points at QR Code.io and decide whether to keep paying until you reprint; dynamic codes rely on the platform.
2. Recreate new codes in the [QRdx playground](/playground), pointing straight at your content.
3. Host files such as PDFs yourself (or in Google Drive) and encode that link.
4. Test each code on a few phones before printing.
