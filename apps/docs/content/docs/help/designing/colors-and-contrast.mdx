---
title: Choose colors that scan
description: Keep a dark code on a light background, keep strong contrast, and use gradients carefully.
---

Scanners look for dark squares on a light background. Most color problems come from breaking that rule. This page shows where the colors are in the playground and how to keep a code scannable while you change them.

## Change the colors

1. In the [playground](/playground?tab=design&section=colors), open the **Design** tab, then **Colors**.
2. Pick the color to change. **Background** is the area behind the code. **Foreground** colors the small squares that hold your data. **Eyes** colors the three big squares in the corners. **Dots** colors the center of each corner square.
3. Choose a fill: **Solid**, **Linear**, **Radial**, **Angular** or **Image**.

The preview and the **Scan score** under it update as you go. **Color palettes**, under the four colors, sets them all at once.

## Rules that keep a code scannable

- **Dark code, light background.** Light codes on dark backgrounds fail in some camera apps.
- **Strong contrast.** Aim for a contrast ratio of 4.5:1 or more between the code and the background.
- **Gradients stay dark.** A gradient is fine when every color in it is dark enough against the background.
- **Plain background.** A busy photo behind the code makes it harder to read. If you want a picture in the code, use [Image QR](/docs/help/designing/art) instead, which tests the result for you.

## Check the contrast

**Contrast Checker** in the toolbar above the preview shows the ratio for your colors and flags pairs under 4.5:1.

The **Scan score** under the preview checks contrast too, along with size, clear space and the logo. See [Check a design with the scan score](/docs/help/designing/scan-score).

## If something goes wrong

### The score says Fails after a color change

The contrast is far too low for a camera to tell the code from the background. Make the foreground darker or the background lighter until the score comes back.

### It scans on your phone but not on other phones

Light-on-dark codes and low-contrast pairs read on some cameras and not others. Swap the colors so the code is the dark part, and bring the ratio to 4.5:1 or more.

### A gradient makes the score Risky

One end of the gradient is too light. Move both colors toward the dark end, or use a solid foreground and keep the gradient for the eyes.
