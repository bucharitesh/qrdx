---
title: Sign in with a passkey
description: Add a passkey to sign in with your fingerprint, face or screen lock, and remove one you no longer use.
---

A passkey signs you in to QRdx with your fingerprint, your face or your device's screen lock. It's saved on your device or in your password manager, and it only works on QRdx's own site, so a look-alike site can't collect it.

## Add a passkey

1. Go to **Settings** → **Profile & security**.
2. Under **Passkeys**, click **Add passkey**.
3. Keep the suggested name (your browser and system, like "Chrome on macOS") or type your own, then click **Continue**.
4. Your browser asks where to save the passkey and checks your fingerprint, face or screen lock.

You need to have signed in within the last 24 hours to add a passkey. If it's been longer, QRdx asks you to sign out and sign in again first.

QRdx emails your account's address whenever a passkey is added or removed.

The passkey has to check that it's you each time it's used. Phones, computers and password managers do this with your fingerprint, face or screen lock. A security key needs a PIN set on it.

## Sign in with a passkey

On the sign-in page, click **Sign in with a passkey** and choose yours. In browsers that support it, your passkeys also appear as suggestions when you click the email field.

A passkey signs in to the account it was added to. It can't create an account: if you're new to QRdx, sign in with Google, GitHub or an email code first, then add a passkey.

## Synced and device-bound passkeys

Settings labels each passkey **Synced** or **Device-bound**.

A synced passkey is backed up to a password manager or a cloud keychain, such as iCloud Keychain or Google Password Manager. It works on every device signed in to that account.

A device-bound passkey stays on the one phone, computer or security key that made it. To sign in somewhere else, use another sign-in method or add a passkey on that device too.

## Rename or remove a passkey

Under **Passkeys**, click **Rename** or **Remove** next to the passkey. A removed passkey stops working on QRdx straight away. Your device or password manager may keep listing it until you delete it there as well.

Lost a device that had a passkey on it? Sign in another way and remove that passkey.

You can always sign in with a code sent to your email, so removing your last passkey doesn't lock you out.

## Passkeys and two-step verification

A passkey sign-in already takes two things: the device that holds the passkey, and your fingerprint, face or screen lock. If you've turned on two-step verification, QRdx doesn't ask for an authenticator code after you sign in with a passkey. Signing in with Google, GitHub or an email code still asks for one.
