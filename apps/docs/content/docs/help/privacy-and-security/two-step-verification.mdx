---
title: Two-step verification
description: Ask for a code from an authenticator app every time you sign in, keep backup codes, and get back in if you lose your phone.
---

Two-step verification (also called 2FA) adds a second step to signing in. After your email code, Google, GitHub or workspace single sign-on, QRdx asks for a 6-digit code from an authenticator app. Someone who gets into your email or your Google account still can't get into QRdx without that code.

It works with any authenticator app that shows 6-digit codes that change every 30 seconds, including the one-time code feature of most password managers. QRdx doesn't send second-step codes by email or text message.

## Turn it on

1. Go to **Settings** → **Profile & security**.
2. Under **Two-step verification**, click **Turn on**.
3. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app. If you can't scan it, type the key shown under it into the app.
4. Enter the 6-digit code the app shows. Two-step verification is now on.
5. Save your 10 backup codes. Copy them or download them as a text file, and keep them somewhere safe, such as a password manager. QRdx shows them only once.

Turning it on needs a sign-in from the last 24 hours. If yours is older, QRdx asks you to sign out and sign in again first.

We email your account's address whenever two-step verification is turned on or off, or new backup codes are made.

## Sign in with it

Sign in the way you always do. QRdx then asks for the code from your authenticator app. Codes change every 30 seconds, so if one is rejected, wait for the next.

Each sign-in step allows 5 wrong codes. After that, start the sign-in again. After 10 wrong codes in a row, QRdx stops accepting codes for your account for 15 minutes.

The step expires after 10 minutes. If it does, sign in again.

## Backup codes

A backup code works instead of an authenticator code, for when you don't have your phone. On the code step, click **Use a backup code** and enter one, like `abcde-12345`. Each code works once.

Settings shows how many codes you have left. To get a fresh set, click **New backup codes** and confirm with a current code. Your old codes stop working.

## Trusted devices

On the code step, tick **Don't ask again on this device for 30 days** to skip the code on that browser for 30 days. Only use it on a device that's yours. Turning two-step verification off forgets every trusted device.

## Which sign-ins ask for a code

All of them: email codes, Google, GitHub and workspace single sign-on.

The one exception is a passkey. Signing in with a passkey that checks your fingerprint, face or device PIN already uses two factors, so QRdx doesn't ask for a code. If the passkey didn't check you that way, QRdx asks for the code as well.

## Turn it off

Go to **Settings** → **Profile & security**, click **Turn off** under **Two-step verification**, and enter a code from your authenticator app or a backup code. Your authenticator setup and backup codes are deleted.

## Lost your phone and your backup codes

Email [support@qrdx.dev](mailto:support@qrdx.dev) from your account's email address and tell us what happened. Once we've confirmed the request came from that address, we turn off two-step verification for your account and email you to say so. You can then sign in as usual and turn it on again with your new phone.
