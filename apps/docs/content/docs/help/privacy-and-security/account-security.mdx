## No password to leak

QRdx has no passwords. You sign in with Google, GitHub or a one-time code sent to your email<IfAvailable feature="passkeys">, or with a passkey</IfAvailable>.<IfAvailable feature="sso"> If your workspace is on the Business plan, it can also let you sign in with your company's [single sign-on](/docs/help/teams/single-sign-on).</IfAvailable>

That makes your email account, and your Google or GitHub account, keys to QRdx. Turn on two-step verification for them too.

## Turn on two-step verification

[Two-step verification](/docs/help/privacy-and-security/two-step-verification) makes QRdx ask for a second step after you sign in, whichever way you sign in: <IfAvailable feature="passkeys">your passkey or </IfAvailable>a code from an authenticator app. Someone who gets into your email or your Google account still can't get into QRdx without it. You get 10 backup codes for when you can't do the second step. Turning it on signs out every other browser or device signed in to your account, so each one has to sign in again with the second step.

<IfAvailable feature="passkeys" title="Add a passkey">
A [passkey](/docs/help/privacy-and-security/passkeys) checks it's you with your fingerprint, face or screen lock. It only works on QRdx's own site, so a look-alike site can't collect it. It can sign you in on its own with **Continue with passkey**, or be your second step. Adding a passkey while two-step verification is off turns it on, which signs out your other devices.
</IfAvailable>

## Changes ask you to confirm it's you

Before a change to your account's security, QRdx asks you to [confirm it's you](/docs/help/privacy-and-security/two-step-verification#confirm-its-you), even though you're signed in: changing your email address, deleting your account, turning two-step verification on or off, new backup codes, changing your second-step methods, or linking or unlinking Google or GitHub. So do creating an API key or an OAuth app's client secret, approving an app that asks to use your account, showing or rotating a webhook's signing secret, and, in a workspace, deleting it, changing someone's role, removing someone or inviting an admin.<IfAvailable feature="sso"> So do changes to a workspace's single sign-on connection.</IfAvailable>

With two-step verification on, you confirm with <IfAvailable feature="passkeys">your passkey, </IfAvailable>an authenticator code or a backup code, never with an emailed code. Without it, you can use a 6-digit code QRdx emails you<IfAvailable feature="passkeys">, your passkey if you have one,</IfAvailable> or sign in again with a Google or GitHub account you've linked<IfAvailable feature="sso"> (or your workspace's single sign-on, when it uses OIDC)</IfAvailable>. A confirmation lasts 10 minutes in that browser.

Changing your email address also needs a code sent to the new address. Afterwards you're signed out everywhere else, and the old address gets an email saying it changed. Lost access to your email? See [what to do](/docs/help/account-and-billing/sign-in#lost-access-to-your-email).

## Emails about your account's security

We email your account's address when:

- a browser or device signs in to your account for the first time;
- two-step verification is turned on or off, an authenticator app<IfAvailable feature="passkeys"> or a passkey</IfAvailable> is added or removed, or new backup codes are made;
- a backup code is used, to sign in or to confirm it's you, with how many codes you have left;
- support is asked to turn off your two-step verification, with the day it happens and how to stop it, and when that's cancelled;
- your email address is changed (to the old address).

Each one says what happened, on which device and browser (like "Chrome on macOS"), roughly where, from which IP address and when. **Review security** opens your security settings. If it wasn't you, follow the steps in the email and contact support.

Emails with a code in them, such as sign-in codes and the code that confirms it's you, say roughly where the code was asked for. QRdx will never ask for a code by phone, chat or email: don't pass one on to anyone.

### New sign-ins

QRdx recognises a browser by a cookie it sets when you finish signing in. When a browser or device without that cookie signs in to your account, you get an email. With two-step verification on, it's sent once the second step is done.

Signing in again in a browser QRdx already recognises doesn't send one, and nor does creating your account. If you clear your cookies or use a private window, the next sign-in there counts as new.

Every new browser gets its own email, up to 10 a day, so a burst of sign-ins can't flood your inbox. Every sign-in still shows under **Sessions**. QRdx remembers the 50 browsers you've used most recently, and forgets one that hasn't signed in for 400 days.

<IfAvailable feature="sso">
One exception: when you sign in through your workspace's SAML identity provider and two-step verification is off, QRdx can't recognise the browser on the way back, so it doesn't send this email.
</IfAvailable>

## How long you stay signed in

A sign-in lasts up to 30 days. After that, QRdx asks you to sign in again in that browser, however often you've used it. If you don't use QRdx in a browser for about a week, you're signed out there sooner.

## Sign out other devices

Go to **Settings** → **Profile & security**. Under **Sessions**, sign out of one device, or click **Sign out everywhere else**. This never asks you to confirm it's you, so you can do it straight away.

Turning on two-step verification signs out every other device too. If support turns it off for you, which [waits 7 days](/docs/help/privacy-and-security/two-step-verification#lost-your-phone-and-your-backup-codes), every device is signed out on that day, including the one you're using.

## Remove apps you connected

If you let another app use your QRdx account, it's listed under **Settings** → **Authorized apps**. Remove any you don't use. An app keeps working after you sign out, so approving one asks you to confirm it's you.

## Report a security problem

Email [support@qrdx.dev](mailto:support@qrdx.dev) with a subject starting `[security]`. Please don't post it in public GitHub issues. See the [security policy](/legal/security).

A harmful QR code isn't a security bug: [report it](/docs/help/privacy-and-security/report-abuse) instead.
