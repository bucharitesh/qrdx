**Short answer: the printed pattern never expires.** A phone can read a QR code for as long as the print is legible. What stops is the link inside it. A static code holds your own address, so it works for as long as that page does. A dynamic code holds a short link that a QR service forwards to your page, so it works only while that service keeps forwarding it. Most codes that "expire" are dynamic codes whose trial or plan ended.

## Static and dynamic codes

A **static QR code** stores the content itself: `https://yourcafe.com/menu`, a Wi-Fi name and password, a contact card. The phone reads it and opens it directly. No service sits in between, so nobody can switch it off.

A **dynamic QR code** stores a short link on a QR service's domain, such as `qrdx.dev/q/aB3dE5f`. When someone scans it, the service looks up where you want it to go and forwards the phone there. That's what lets you change the destination after printing and count scans. It's also why the code depends on the service. The forwarding happens only while the service keeps doing it, and on many services that depends on your account.

Providers describe it the same way. One provider's own guide says dynamic codes "can become inactive if a free trial ends, a paid plan lapses, or you reach platform limits" ([QR Code Generator PRO blog](https://www.qr-code-generator.com/blog/do-qr-codes-expire/)).

## First, find out which kind you have

Scan the code with your phone's camera and read the address it shows before you tap it. That first address tells you what the code holds.

- **Your own website, or the content itself** (a Wi-Fi network name, a contact): it's a static code. It can't be switched off, so if it fails, the problem is the page behind it.
- **A short link on a QR service's domain**: it's a dynamic code. Whether it works depends on that service and your account there.
- **A short link on your own domain**, like `go.yourcafe.com/menu`: it's a dynamic code on a domain you control, so you decide where it points.

If your phone shows no preview, take a photo of the code and drop it into the [scan checker](/tools/scan-checker). It shows exactly what the code holds.

Then open it and note where you land. A page from the QR service saying the code is inactive, paused or needs an upgrade means the forwarding was switched off. An error page from your own website means the page moved. If nothing opens at all, the print is the likely problem.

## Why a printed QR code stops working

### The free trial ended

Several dynamic QR services start you on a free trial of a week or two. On some of them, the dynamic codes you made during the trial stop when it ends. Here's how three providers put it on their own pages:

- "Any Dynamic QR Codes that you created during the trial period will be deactivated and linked to a service page." ([QRFY help](https://support.qrfy.com/en/articles/6998440-what-happens-to-my-account-and-qr-codes-when-the-trial-expires))
- When the free trial expires, "Dynamic QR codes will be paused." ([QR.io help](https://help.qr.io/en/articles/6977506-what-happens-to-my-qr-codes-and-account-when-the-free-trial-expires))
- "Your Dynamic QR Code will stop working once the free trial ends." ([Scanova pricing](https://scanova.io/pricing))

That's how a menu or flyer code can work for a week and then stop: it was made during a trial and printed before the trial ran out.

### The plan lapsed or was canceled

On many services, dynamic codes work only while the account is paid. QR TIGER's FAQ says of its paid plans: "If your plan expires, it will stop working" ([QR TIGER FAQ](https://www.qrcode-tiger.com/faq)). Uniqode's help center says: "Your dynamic QR Codes will only expire if your Uniqode account is canceled" ([Uniqode help](https://docs.uniqode.com/en/articles/7435358-when-will-my-qr-codes-expire)).

Not every service works this way. Flowcode's help center says that after a downgrade to its Basic tier, the codes you no longer have access to "will continue to scan" ([Flowcode help](https://help.flowcode.com/en/articles/6927502-what-happens-to-my-flowcodes-when-i-cancel)). Hovercode says your first 3 codes "will never expire", while extra codes made on a paid plan "will expire if your subscription is cancelled" ([Hovercode pricing](https://hovercode.com/pricing/)).

### A scan cap or code cap was reached

Some free plans count scans. QRCodeChimp's free plan has 1,000 scans a month across all dynamic codes, and past that, "scans are paused" until the next month ([QRCodeChimp pricing](https://www.qrcodechimp.com/pricing)). QR TIGER's free plan has 3 dynamic codes with "a 500 scan limit each" ([QR TIGER FAQ](https://www.qrcode-tiger.com/faq)). A code on a busy poster can reach a limit like that fast.

Some plans count codes. On QRCodeChimp, an account whose paid plan isn't renewed moves to Free, and "the dynamic codes other than the last 10 will be paused" (same page).

### The page behind it is gone

Sometimes the code is fine and the destination isn't. A static code that points at `yourcafe.com/menu-2025.pdf` breaks the day someone renames the file. The same thing happens when a website is rebuilt, a domain registration lapses or a dynamic code's destination is deleted.

### It isn't the link at all

A code that opens nothing, not even a wrong page, usually has a print problem. It may be too small for the scanning distance, too low in contrast, shiny enough to catch the light, faded from sun, or covered too much by a logo. Check it with the [scan checker](/tools/scan-checker) and the [print size calculator](/tools/print-size).

## My QR code stopped working: what to do

Pick the case that matches what you found.

### A dynamic code on a QR service's short link

The short link belongs to the service, so only that service can make it forward again. No other generator can change where another service's short link goes, and that includes QRdx. One provider's guide says the same: a dynamic code "depends on the original provider's redirection domain" ([QR Code Generator PRO blog](https://www.qr-code-generator.com/blog/do-qr-codes-expire/)).

Your options, from quickest to longest-lasting:

1. **Pay the provider.** Many keep deactivated codes and switch them back on when the account is paid again. QRFY says upgrading lets you "reactivate your Dynamic QR Codes" ([QRFY help](https://support.qrfy.com/en/articles/6998440-what-happens-to-my-account-and-qr-codes-when-the-trial-expires)), and QR.io says the same ([QR.io help](https://help.qr.io/en/articles/6977506-what-happens-to-my-qr-codes-and-account-when-the-free-trial-expires)). Don't wait too long: QR TIGER keeps data from an expired plan "for a maximum period of 1 year" ([QR TIGER FAQ](https://www.qrcode-tiger.com/faq)). If you need the codes only until you reprint, ask whether there's a monthly plan.
2. **Ask them.** Write to support with the code's short link, where it's printed and how long you need it. Ask what they can offer, such as a short extension or a smaller plan that covers that one code. Get the answer in writing.
3. **Reprint as a static code.** If the destination won't change (a menu page on your own site, a contact card, Wi-Fi details), a static code needs no account and can't be switched off. Before you print, scan the downloaded file and check that it opens your own address, not a short link.
4. **Reprint with a dynamic code you control.** If you need to change the link later or count scans, read what the service does when a trial ends, a plan lapses or a code gets popular before you choose it. Better still, put the short link on your own domain, as the next case explains.

### A dynamic code on your own domain

If the short link uses a domain you own, like `go.yourcafe.com`, you decide where that domain points. You can stay with the current provider, or point the domain at another service that supports custom domains and set up the same paths there (`/menu`, for example). Scanners won't notice, and nothing gets reprinted. Check the exact paths on your printed codes first, because the new service has to answer at exactly the same addresses.

### A static code whose page moved

Put the page back, or add a redirect on your website from the old address to the new one. If your domain lapsed, renew it. The printed code works again without a reprint.

### A dynamic code whose destination broke

Sign in to the service, change the code's destination, save, and scan it to check.

### A print problem

Reprint it larger, with more contrast and a matte finish. [How big should a QR code be?](/blog/how-big-should-a-qr-code-be) has the numbers.

## How to avoid it on your next print run

- **Use a static code when the content won't change.** Wi-Fi details, contact cards, payment details and links to stable pages on your own site don't need a dynamic code. Static codes are free and keep working for as long as the content they hold is valid.
- **Scan the file before you print it.** If it opens a short link on the generator's domain, it's dynamic, whatever the page called it. Make sure that's what you wanted.
- **Read the policy before you print a dynamic code.** Find out what happens when the trial ends, when you cancel, when a payment fails and when a code gets popular. Words like "deactivated", "paused", "inactive" or "service page" mean the code depends on your plan.
- **Put dynamic codes on your own domain.** A short link like `go.yourcafe.com/menu` can move with you. The next time you change providers, you point the domain somewhere else instead of reprinting.
- **Keep a list of your printed codes.** Note where each one is printed, what it holds and which account it belongs to, so you know where to look when one breaks.

## FAQ

### Do QR codes expire?

The pattern doesn't. A static code works for as long as the content it holds is valid. A dynamic code works for as long as the service behind its short link keeps forwarding it, which on many services depends on a trial or a paid plan.

### Do free QR codes expire?

Free static codes don't, whichever generator made them. Free dynamic codes depend on the service: some stop when a trial ends, some pause after a scan limit, and some keep working on a free plan.

### Is there a free QR code that doesn't expire?

Yes, a static code. It holds your link or content directly, so there's no account to lapse. Scan the file before you print it to make sure it's static. If you need a dynamic code, read the provider's policy on trials and cancellations first.

### Can a deactivated QR code be reactivated?

Often, by the service that made it. Many keep deactivated codes and switch them back on when the account is paid again. Ask before you delete anything.

### Can another QR generator fix my code?

No. A dynamic code's short link belongs to the service whose domain it uses, and only that service can change where it goes. The exception is a short link on your own domain: you can point that domain at another service.

### Does a QR code expire if nobody scans it?

No. Neither kind stops from lack of use, though a service could remove codes from a closed or deleted account.

### How long does a printed QR code last?

As long as the print stays legible. Indoors, that's many years. Outdoors, sun and weather are the limits, so use UV-stable ink, a laminate and a slightly larger code.
